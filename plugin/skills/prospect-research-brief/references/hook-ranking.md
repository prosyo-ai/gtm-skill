# Hook ranking

## The grid

| | Connects to your problem | Does not connect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Specific to the person** | Use it. Lead with it. | Use only as a light aside, never as the opener. |
| **Generic (role or company level)** | Use when nothing better exists. | Drop it. |

## Hook strength, strongest first

1. **Their stated opinion on the problem you solve.** A post, a talk, a quote in an article.
2. **A change in their remit.** New role, new team, new region, new target.
3. **A project they are visibly running.** Hiring for it, posting about it, speaking about it.
4. **A company trigger that lands on their desk.** Funding with growth targets for a VP Sales.
5. **A peer comparison.** A similar company solved this; name it only if the claim is approved.
6. **Role-level insight.** What people in their role usually wrestle with at their company's stage.

## Strong and weak versions

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "Loved your recent post!" | "Your post on cutting ramp time to 45 days made a point I rarely see: that most of it is list quality, not coaching." |
| "Congrats on the new role!" | "Three months into running RevOps at Lumen is usually when the tool audit starts." |
| "I see you went to Michigan." | (Drop it unless the message is about Michigan.) |
| "Saw you're hiring." | "Four SDR listings in two weeks, all mentioning outbound from scratch, suggests the team is being rebuilt." |

## When there is nothing

About a third of prospects have no public activity. Do not fake it. Use the role-and-trigger fallback:

"Most heads of operations at multi-site groups your size tell us payroll closes take two to three days. Is that true at Northwind too?"

It is honest, it is relevant and it invites a correction, which is still a reply.
