# LinkedIn sequence example with reply branches

Persona: Heads of Finance at 50-200 person companies. Topic: month-end close.

**Connection note**
> Your post on cutting close from 10 days to 6 had some smart ideas. Would be good to connect.

**Message 1 (2 days after accept)**
> Thanks for connecting, Maya. Curious: of the ideas in your post, which one actually saved the most time? The approval cut-off one surprised me.

**Message 2 (5 days later, if no reply)**
> One thing I keep hearing from finance leads at your size: the accounting is fast, it's the last-week approvals that drag. A controller at Contoso fixed it with a day-3 cut-off and one approval queue, and got to a 4-day close. Does that match what you see?

**Message 3 (6 days later)**
> If it's useful, I can send the checklist Contoso used. It's a one-pager, no strings. Want it?

**Message 4 (8 days later)**
> Will leave it here, Maya. If close time becomes a priority again, happy to compare notes anytime.

## Reply branches

| Their reply | Your answer |
| --- | --- |
| Answers the question (engaged) | Respond to what they said first; then share one relevant insight; ask if the problem is live for them now |
| "Sure, send it" | Send the asset, then: "Happy to walk you through how they rolled it out, 15 minutes this week or next?" |
| "What do you do?" | One sentence of what you do and for whom, one proof point, then a question about their situation |
| "Not a priority" | "Makes sense, thanks for saying so. Mind if I check back in a quarter?" |
| "We already use a tool for that" | "Got it. Out of curiosity, how long does close take with it today?" (only if genuinely curious; otherwise thank them) |
| "Not interested" / "Stop" | "Understood, I won't message again." Then stop everywhere. |

## Timing guardrails

- Send during their working hours, weekdays.
- No more than one unanswered message every 4 days.
- Total LinkedIn messages without a reply: 4 at most.
