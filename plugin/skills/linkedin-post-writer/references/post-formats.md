# Post formats with examples

## Story

```
Hook: the moment, specific and vivid
Tension: what was at stake
Turn: what changed
Lesson: one sentence the reader can use
Question: invite their version
```

> Our best customer almost churned in month two.
>
> Their ops lead, Priya, emailed: "We're spending more time on this than on the old spreadsheet."
>
> We had built a beautiful onboarding. She needed one thing: her existing shift codes imported.
>
> We did it by hand that afternoon. She renewed for three years.
>
> Lesson: the fastest onboarding is the one that starts from the customer's mess, not your template.
>
> What's the one thing that made you stick with a tool?

## Lesson list

> I've reviewed 400 cold emails this year. The ones that got replies had five things in common:
>
> 1. Under 90 words
> 2. One problem, not three
> 3. A named peer, not "leading companies"
> 4. No links in the first email
> 5. An ask you can answer with one word
>
> The ones that failed usually started with "I hope this finds you well".
>
> Which of these do you break most often?

## Contrarian take

> Hiring more SDRs won't fix your pipeline.
>
> Most teams that double SDR headcount see meetings per rep drop by a third. Same list, more people fishing in it.
>
> The fix is upstream: better accounts, better timing, better first lines.
>
> Before you open four reqs, check how many accounts in your ICP haven't been touched this quarter.
>
> Agree or disagree?

## How-to

Hook with the result, then numbered steps, then the outcome and a question.

## Behind the scenes

What we tried, the numbers, what surprised us, what we'd do differently. Honest numbers, including the bad ones, build the most trust.
