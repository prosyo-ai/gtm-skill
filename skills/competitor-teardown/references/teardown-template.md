# Teardown template

## Compete matrix

Score each segment from your deal data: win rate against this competitor, and number of deals.

| Segment | Deals | Win rate vs them | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SMB, self-serve | 22 | 25% | Avoid or qualify hard | Their free tier and instant setup |
| Mid-market, multi-team | 31 | 58% | Compete | Our admin controls and reporting |
| Enterprise, regulated | 6 | 50% | Compete with proof | Small sample; lead with security pack |

Verdict rules: under 35% win rate on 10+ deals is **avoid**; 35-50% is **compete with a specific play**; over 50% is **compete**. Under 10 deals is **unknown**, so test before deciding.

## Complaint clustering

| Theme | Mentions | Example (paraphrased) | Source type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow support | 14 | "Tickets sit for a week" | Reviews |

Only themes with 5+ mentions go into the teardown as weaknesses.

## Talk track rules

- Acknowledge, then differentiate: "They are a good fit for teams that want X. Teams that need Y tend to pick us because..."
- Use customer words, not adjectives.
- One proof point per claim: a customer story, a number, a demo moment.

## Landmine question bank

- "How long did the last team your size take to go live?"
- "What happens to the price at renewal once you add seats?"
- "How do you report on <the thing they are weak at> today?"
- "Who on your team owns the tool day to day, and how many hours a week does it take?"

Each question should be fair to ask even if the answer favours the competitor.
