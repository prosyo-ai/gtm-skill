# Cold call brush-offs

Pattern: **acknowledge → one question → respect the answer**.

| Brush-off | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Just send me an email." | "Happy to. So I send something useful rather than a brochure, is <problem> actually something you're dealing with?" |
| "We're all set." / "We already have something." | "Good to hear. Out of curiosity, how long does <outcome> take with what you have today?" |
| "Not interested." | "Fair enough. Is that because <problem> isn't an issue, or just not a priority right now?" |
| "I'm busy right now." | "Understood. Is there a better 2 minutes later this week, or should I not call back?" |
| "How did you get my number?" | Answer honestly with the source type (a business contact database, the company website), and offer to remove it. |
| "What's this about?" (sceptical) | Give the reason in one sentence and ask the problem question. |
| "We don't have budget." | "Makes sense. When does planning for next year start? I'd rather be useful then than pushy now." |
| "Talk to <someone else>." | "Thanks. What should I know about how <name> thinks about this before I call?" |
| "Call me next quarter." | "Will do. What changes next quarter that makes it a better time?" Then book the callback date. |
| "Is this a sales call?" | "Yes, it is. Can I take 30 seconds to say why, and you decide?" |

## Voicemail after a no-answer

Under 20 seconds: name, company, one-line reason tied to the email, "I'll send you a note too", number said twice slowly. Expect few callbacks; the voicemail's job is to make the next email familiar.

## Practice drill (15 minutes)

1. Read the opener aloud five times until it sounds like you.
2. Partner plays the prospect with a random brush-off from the table; respond, then switch.
3. Record one full role-play and listen for: talk ratio, filler words, whether the ask came with two time options.
