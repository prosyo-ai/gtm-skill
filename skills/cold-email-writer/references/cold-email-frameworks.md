# Cold email frameworks

## Four frames that work

| Frame | Structure | Best when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation → Problem → Proof → Ask | Default | You have a trigger or sharp research |
| Problem → Agitate → Solve (short) | Name the pain, show its cost, hint at the fix | Pain is common and costly for the role |
| Before → After → Bridge | Their likely current state, a peer's better state, how | You have a strong customer story |
| Question-led | A single relevant question, then one line of context | Very senior readers, founders |

## Annotated example

> Subject: new sdr team
>
> Hi Dana, Lumen has four SDR roles open, and the listing says outbound is being built from scratch. ← observation, sourced
>
> New teams usually lose their first month to list building: reps researching accounts instead of talking to them. ← problem, in the reader's world
>
> Northwind had the same setup last spring; their reps booked first meetings in week two instead of week six. ← proof, specific
>
> Worth a look before your new hires start? ← low-friction ask

72 words. One problem. One proof. One-word answer possible.

## Weak to strong rewrites

**Weak opener**: "I hope this email finds you well. My name is Sam and I'm with Acme, the leading AI platform for..."
**Strong**: "Saw the Series B post: doubling the sales team by Q4."

**Weak problem**: "Many companies struggle with efficiency and productivity in their sales processes."
**Strong**: "New reps spend their first month building lists instead of selling."

**Weak proof**: "We work with leading companies across many industries."
**Strong**: "Contoso cut ramp from 5 months to 3."

**Weak ask**: "Do you have 30 minutes this week for a demo?"
**Strong**: "Worth a look?" / "Is that on your radar?" / "Want me to send the two-minute version?"

## Line-by-line edit checklist

1. Delete the first sentence. Is the email better? Then keep it deleted.
2. Every sentence earns its place or goes.
3. Replace adjectives with numbers or names.
4. Swap "we help companies" for what the reader gets.
5. One question mark in the whole email.
6. No links, no attachments in email one.
7. Read aloud; anything you would not say to a peer gets rewritten.

## Personalization slots for segment templates

Keep the template 80% fixed and leave one slot of 1-2 sentences for the personal line. A slot that says `{trigger_sentence}` with a fallback line for when research finds nothing scales better than templates with five merge fields.
